This report documents the independent inspection of the raw synthetic dataset
generated by `python/generate_data.py`, run **after** fixing the two bugs
described in `DECISIONS.md`. Numbers below are from the corrected run.

## 2. Referential integrity

- 0 `Product_ID` values in the fact table missing from `dim_product`.

## 3. Controlled data-quality issues — actual vs. target

| Issue type | Target | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicate rows | 0.5–1% | 0.7% (137 of 19,579 pre-injection rows re-inserted) |
| Formatting inconsistencies (Currency casing, PO_Status casing) | 1–2% | 1.5% |
| Missing values (Unit_Price, Quantity, Expected_Delivery_Date) | 1–2% | 1.5% |
| Invalid values (negative price, zero/negative quantity) | <0.5% | 0.39% |
| Invalid dates (malformed Order_Date, Actual before Order) | small number | 13–14 rows each |

All within or very close to target ranges. These are logged as errors, not
business findings — they're candidates for the cleaning step (SQL script
`01_data_quality.sql`), not for interpretation.

Separately, `Actual_Delivery_Date` is blank for ~9% of rows — this is **not**
a data-quality defect, it's the expected/correct state for Open and Cancelled
orders (they haven't been delivered). This matches the PO_Status distribution
almost exactly and should be excluded from "missing value" cleanup — it's
already handled by the "completed orders only, valid dates only" inclusion
rule in PROJECT_SPEC section 14.

## 5. Business realism checks

**Department purchasing volume** (PO line count, high → low):
Manufacturing (3,145) > IT (2,862) > Operations (2,758) > Engineering (2,633)
> Marketing/Facilities/Sales (~1,870–1,930) > Finance (1,350) > HR (1,292).

This matches the intended tiering exactly (Manufacturing/IT/Operations/
Engineering high; Facilities/Sales/Marketing medium; Finance/HR low).

**Department → category affinity** (row-normalized %, selected):

- IT: 51.6% IT Equipment, 33.8% Software — makes sense.
- Facilities: 60.8% Facilities services, 0% IT Equipment — makes sense.
- HR: 36.7% Software, 37.3% Professional Services, 8.7% IT Equipment — makes sense.
- Manufacturing (dept): 11.4% Facilities, spend concentrated in Manufacturing
  category products (components/production supplies) as expected.

No department is buying obviously nonsensical products.

**Spend (completed orders only, valid Unit_Price/Quantity):**

- Total 2-year completed spend: ~$503.4M (~$251.7M/yr)
- Completed PO count: 8,014 | Avg PO value: $62,820 | Median PO value: $34,059
- Spend by category: Manufacturing $204.1M > Software $140.5M > Professional
  Services $52.3M > IT Equipment $50.1M > Office Supplies $22.0M > Facilities
  $18.4M > Logistics $15.9M

This scale is plausible for a ~1,000-employee company with in-house
manufacturing/assembly (component and materials procurement inflates spend
well past a typical office-only company) — but it's a judgment call, not a
fact, and worth a sanity-check gut-check once we're deeper into analysis. If
it looks too high once we build the Executive Overview dashboard, the
`CATEGORY_MAX_LINE_VALUE` ceilings in the generator are a single, clearly
labeled place to tune it down.

**Vendor concentration:** top 5 of 41 vendors account for 35.1% of completed
spend — a believable moderate concentration, not an artificially engineered
"one vendor dominates everything" story (per section 17, we didn't want to
predetermine this finding, and we didn't).

**Delivery performance:** 75.8% on-time overall (n=17,543 valid completed
lines with both dates); average delay among late orders is 12.7 days.
Vendor-level on-time rates range from 52.3% to 97.2% (std dev ~12.5 points) —
a spread, not a bimodal "good vendor / bad vendor" split, matching the
explicit design intent in section 11.

**Price variance:** spot-checked a product sold by 3+ vendors (a laptop
model, standard cost $2,126): observed vendor average unit prices ranged
from $1,912 to $2,223 — a believable ~15% spread driven by vendor pricing
tendency, not identical prices across vendors and not wild outliers.

## 6. Verdict

All technical and business realism checks pass. Two real generator bugs were
found and fixed (see DECISIONS.md) rather than patched in the output. This
dataset is a reasonable candidate to **lock as Checkpoint 2**, pending your
sign-off — in particular your gut check on the ~$250M/yr total spend figure,
since that's the one number here that rests on a judgment call rather than a
hard business questions from the spec.
